================================================================================
REGATTA: SINGLE-DATABASE vs. TWO-DATABASE PIPELINE COMPARISON
Worked dataset: Galapagos MiFish eDNA (1,438 ASVs)
Regional checklist: GBIF + OBIS + local Pisces checklist, taxonomized to NCBI
================================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
Quantify how REGATTA's two pipeline options differ on the same data:

  - SINGLE-DB : one classifier (obitools vs. the global NCBI database),
                reconciled against the regional checklist
                [reconcile_checklist].

  - TWO-DB    : the same ASVs also classified with vsearch+SINTAX against a
                locally-curated reference database; the two classifier runs are
                reconciled against each other [reconcile_global_local] and then
                against the regional checklist [reconcile_checklist].

All numbers below are from the standard pipeline (run_regatta defaults:
Local_advantage = TRUE; percent identity carried to 3 significant figures).


TABLE 1. Final taxonomic rank assigned, per method (n = 1,438 ASVs)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  Final rank        Single-DB     Two-DB      Difference
  ----------------  ----------    --------    ------------
  species                 287        295            +8
  genus                   229        231            +2
  family                   16         19            +3
  order                     1          1             0
  class                     9          0            -9
  unassigned              896        892            -4
  ----------------  ----------    --------    ------------
  total assigned          542        546            +4


KEY FINDINGS
------------
1. Species-level resolution.
   The two-database method reaches species-level slightly MORE often than the
   single-database method (295 vs. 287 ASVs, +8), and assigns 4 more ASVs
   overall (546 vs. 542). It pushes calls down the tree, not up.

2. Average percent identity.
   The backing percent identity of the final call is effectively identical
   between methods (single-DB 98.31%, two-DB 98.30%). The locally-curated
   database is not systematically higher-identity; both classifiers find ~98%
   matches. The benefit of the second database is WHICH taxon it supports, not
   how strong the match is.

3. Most notable effect: the two-database method clears the "class-level tail."
   The single-database method leaves 9 ASVs stranded at class Actinopteri --
   fish the global database could only place to an order/family absent from the
   regional checklist. With the local curated database weighing in, ALL 9
   resolve to family or deeper (class 9 -> 0). The regional reference rescues
   precisely the calls the global database handled worst.


HOW THE TWO-DATABASE RECONCILIATION BEHAVED (546 reconciled ASVs)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  Outcome                                                 ASVs
  ----------------------------------------------------    ----
  global classifier won outright (agree / vsearch silent)  358
  local classifier won (higher %ID, or tie)                143
  global won %ID but local disagreed -> downgraded to the
    lowest common ancestor of the two (conservative)        45

  Among the 542 ASVs assigned by BOTH methods:
    became MORE specific under two-DB                         26
    became LESS specific under two-DB                         24
    unchanged rank                                           492

  Net: the second database trades a small number of over-confident species
  calls for genus when the two classifiers conflict (the 45 LCA downgrades),
  while rescuing slightly more calls than it loses -- yielding more species
  calls and no dead-end class-level assignments.


CAVEAT: SENSITIVITY TO PERCENT-IDENTITY TIE-BREAKING
----------------------------------------------------
The two-database result depends mildly on how percent identities are compared.
Carrying %ID at full numeric precision (instead of the 3-significant-figure
rounding the standard pipeline applies) flips ~17 borderline ASVs between
"local won" and "global -> LCA," changing the species count from 295 to 278.
ASVs where the global and local matches are within rounding of each other are
decided by the tie-break rule (Local_advantage) and are therefore the least
stable calls. The table above reports the standard-pipeline (rounded) values.
